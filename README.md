# NIST CSF 2.0 — MSP Cybersecurity GRC Assessment

**Fictional portfolio case study | Summit Technology Solutions | October 2026**

A risk-informed NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 assessment of selected cybersecurity outcomes in a simulated Denver managed service provider (MSP). This project documents the end-to-end workflow from scoping and evidence review through risk treatment, remediation validation, organizational profiles, and executive reporting.

> **Important:** All organization names, interviews, records, findings, metrics, and management decisions are fictional. This is an educational simulation, **not** an independent audit, formal attestation, certification, or claim of NIST compliance.

## Business scenario
Summit Technology Solutions is a fictional MSP with approximately 150 employees and 75 clients, using Microsoft 365, Azure, ConnectWise, endpoint protection and security monitoring. The assessment focuses on Summit's **internal MSP security operations** and shared administrative infrastructure, not an audit of each client's environment.

## Key results
- Identified an initially critical privileged-access issue involving two unmanaged privileged accounts; authorized containment reduced immediate exposure, but recurring access reviews still need effectiveness testing.
- Identified unreconciled ConnectWise (420) and Defender (455) inventory records. **The difference does not prove 35 devices are missing**; scope, duplicates and lifecycle status must be reconciled.
- Identified limited evidence of incident-response exercises, monitoring validation and backup restoration testing.
- Proposed a risk-informed 90-day improvement roadmap, subject to leadership approval and technical validation.

## Assessment method
1. Establish boundaries, objectives and stakeholders.
2. Review simulated evidence against selected CSF 2.0 outcomes.
3. Classify outcomes as Achieved / Partially achieved / Not achieved / Not verified using a **project-defined** rubric (not NIST-issued scores).
4. Document limitations, current state and measurable target outcomes.
5. Record risks using an illustrative 5×5 likelihood × impact matrix; distinguish preliminary from validated residual risk.
6. Assign remediation ownership, testing criteria, due dates and reporting cadence.

## Caveats and mapping
The assessment addresses **six selected outcomes**, not the full CSF Core. Outcome descriptions are Summit-specific paraphrases and should be checked against the authoritative NIST CSF 2.0 Core. Backup restoration assurance and incident response exercises are tested as business requirements; the mapped CSF outcomes do not necessarily capture every associated practice. NIST does not certify organizations to CSF 2.0.

Official reference: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework

## Interview talking points
This simulated engagement demonstrates assessment scoping, evidence traceability, design-versus-operating-effectiveness testing, risk-based prioritization, risk acceptance governance, Current/Target Profiles, and executive reporting. Describe it explicitly as a **hands-on simulated project** rather than professional audit experience.
